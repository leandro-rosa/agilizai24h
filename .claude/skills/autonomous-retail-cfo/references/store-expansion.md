# Store Decisions: Open / Close / Renegotiate / Expand

Every store decision follows
[financial-decision-framework.md](financial-decision-framework.md). This
reference adds what's specific to each decision type.

## OPEN

Analyze: required CAPEX (equipment, initial fit-out), initial inventory
(working capital, not CAPEX), expected revenue potential, expected gross
margin, expected contribution margin, working capital requirement (see
[working-capital.md](working-capital.md)), total cash requirement (CAPEX +
initial inventory + ramp-period funding gap), break-even revenue/
transactions ([store-unit-economics.md](store-unit-economics.md)), payback,
ROIC, and a downside case. Use
[templates/investment-decision.md](../templates/investment-decision.md).

Revenue potential for a not-yet-opened store is always an ESTIMATE — never
present it as a fact even when it's built from a careful comparable-store
analysis. State the comparable(s) used.

## CLOSE

Before recommending closure, evaluate whether the underlying problem is
fixable and cheaper than closing:

- Are the losses temporary (ramp period, one-off disruption) or structural?
- **Fixed vs. avoidable cost** — closing only helps by the amount of cost
  that actually goes away; allocated corporate overhead mostly doesn't (see
  [store-unit-economics.md](store-unit-economics.md)). Build the close case
  on Store Contribution, not Fully Allocated Store Profit.
- Location commission terms, supplier conditions, shrinkage rate,
  assortment fit, stockout rate, pricing, logistics cost, revenue trend, and
  store maturity (a store <6-12 months old is still ramping, not failed).
- Exit costs — remaining lease/commission obligations, equipment
  redeployment or write-off, unwound inventory.

Compare, side by side, before recommending Close:

```
Close  vs  Renegotiate  vs  Resize  vs  Relocate  vs  Improve assortment  vs  Change pricing
```

Each option gets its own Store Contribution estimate and cash impact — a
close recommendation that hasn't priced the alternatives isn't done.

## RENEGOTIATE

Evaluate whether changing condominium/location commission, rent, supplier
price, payment terms, logistics cost, payment processor, or software cost
would restore acceptable Store Contribution. Quantify the specific
improvement needed (a decision threshold) before starting a renegotiation
conversation — "we need the commission down by at least X pp to clear a
Y% contribution margin."

## EXPAND

Capital allocation is comparative, not a yes/no on one project. Compare the
incremental contribution / incremental free cash flow of:

```
new stores  vs  existing-store improvements  vs  inventory  vs
supplier discounts (see supplier-economics.md)  vs  technology  vs
cash reserve  vs  debt repayment
```

Rank by ROIC/payback where comparable; flag where capital availability
(not just returns) is the binding constraint — see
[cash-management.md](cash-management.md).
