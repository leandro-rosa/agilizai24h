## Decisions

1. **Category key stays the stable identifier.** `product.category` keeps holding a slug key (`meal`, `snack`, `beverage`, `essential`, and any new one); the new `category` table owns the key, the display name, keywords and status. The four existing keys are seeded unchanged, so nothing that stores or filters by key (finance, supply, pricing margin overrides by category) is touched. A new category gets a slug key from its name, unique and immutable; renaming changes only the name.
2. **Subcategory stays a text on the product**, now validated against the `subcategory` table (belongs to the product's category, active). Renaming a subcategory renames it on its products in one transaction; moving one to another category is refused while products use it. The eight existing texts are seeded under the category they already have; no product changes.
3. **Never delete.** There is no delete route. A used category or subcategory is inactivated: it stops being offered for new products and keeps working for the products that have it, and the history is untouched.
4. **Classification is deterministic.** Keywords and synonyms are matched against the folded name (phrases count by words). A subcategory match implies its category. One clear winner fills category and subcategory; a tie between different targets is "ambiguous" and returns the alternatives; no match returns nothing. It never creates a category and never learns on its own. Seed keywords exist only for the eight existing subcategories and the four categories and are editable.
5. **Confirmed classification.** `product.classification_confirmed` is true when a person saved the category through a form. Import and invoice flows classify only products that are new or not confirmed, and say when they kept a confirmed one.
6. **Review, not reclassification.** `GET /classification/review` lists proposals (products without subcategory or unconfirmed whose proposal differs); `POST /classification/apply` applies only the items the operator selected and marks them confirmed.
7. **Draft suggestion.** The pricing engine already builds the cost structure from shared components. A draft read reuses it for a product that is not registered: category loss (no product history), payment mix, tax, operating share, target and rounding, the same `solveStructure` as the report and the new-product suggestion. The form sends category and unit cost (and optionally a typed price to get its margin); the response lists what is missing instead of a number. No sales volume, impact or confidence beyond "initial" is produced.
8. **Unit cost** is the package cost divided by the conversion factor, rounded to the centavo (the rule already used by the invoice import); the form shows it and sends it.
9. **Save vs approve.** Saving the registration creates the product, its EANs and its first cost version (manual, with the reason "custo informado no cadastro"); approving the price is a second explicit action through the existing price-choice path (recorded with user, validity and previous price). "Save with price pending" stops after the first.
10. **One area, old routes redirect.** `/products` hosts the three views (`?view=catalog|pricing|categories`, catalog default; the existing `tab` and `sku` parameters keep meaning the product drawer). `/purchases/pricing` redirects to `?view=pricing`, preserving `sku`. The menu has one entry.
11. **Out of scope** (shown in the mockups, not requested in the text): the "Margem e Markup" view, per-store price selection and any POS publication (there is no integration that would confirm it).
